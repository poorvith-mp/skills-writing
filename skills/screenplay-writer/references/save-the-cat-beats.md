# Save the Cat 15-beat structure (feature-length reference)

Use as a scaffold for outlining, not a rigid formula to force every story into — the percentages are for a ~110-page script and should scale proportionally for shorter/longer projects.

1. **Opening Image** (p. 1) — a snapshot that captures the story's "before" state.
2. **Theme Stated** (p. 5) — someone states the story's thematic question, usually not the protagonist.
3. **Setup** (p. 1-10) — introduce the hero's world, flaws, and what's missing.
4. **Catalyst** (p. 12) — the inciting incident that disrupts the status quo.
5. **Debate** (p. 12-25) — the hero hesitates; should they go?
6. **Break into Two** (p. 25) — the hero commits and leaves the old world behind.
7. **B Story** (p. 30) — a secondary story (often a relationship) that carries the theme.
8. **Fun and Games** (p. 30-55) — the "promise of the premise" — the trailer-moment scenes.
9. **Midpoint** (p. 55) — a false victory or false defeat that raises the stakes.
10. **Bad Guys Close In** (p. 55-75) — external and internal pressure mounts.
11. **All Is Lost** (p. 75) — the lowest point, often marked by a "whiff of death."
12. **Dark Night of the Soul** (p. 75-85) — the hero processes the loss before finding resolve.
13. **Break into Three** (p. 85) — the hero finds the solution, combining lessons from the A and B stories.
14. **Finale** (p. 85-110) — the hero executes the plan and resolves the conflict.
15. **Final Image** (p. 110) — a mirror or contrast to the Opening Image, showing transformation.
